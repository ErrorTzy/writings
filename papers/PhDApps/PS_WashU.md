**Areas of Interest:** My primary interests are in the philosophy of mind and language. Specifically, I am interested in the nature of linguistic thoughts and non-linguistic representations, as well as intentionality. I also have a strong interest in metaphysics, esp. empirical and perceptual basis of ontological commitment, and metaphysics of agency. I hope to pursue these interests along with the interpretability and alignment of LLMs. I believe that investigating the interpretation of LLMs can shed light on how to interpret human language and thought on the one hand, while contributing to our ability to understand and align these models on the other. WashU is an ideal place for me, given the strong faculty in philosophy of mind, cognitive science and psychology. In particular, I share a commitment to taking an empirical approach to questions traditionally treated as a priori.

**Previous Coursework and Experience:** During my M.A. at Brandeis, I was deeply influenced by my advisor, Eli Hirsch. Under his influence I developed an interest in the relationship between meaning, interpretation, and ontological commitment. My M.A. thesis, which is my sample paper, argues against Hirsch’s metaphysical deflationism. I argue that Hirschian deflationism relies on a questionable application of the metasemantic principle of interpretive charity, and that the deflationary move that does not invoke this principle risks circularity. In doctoral study, I hope to connect this conclusion to Hirsch's earlier work on developmental psychology and infant object individuation.

In another paper written for Hirsch’s seminar, I argued against Cappelen and Dever that the indexicality of the first-person pronoun is essentially irreducible for rational agents. I constructed an alternative, indexical-free language whose speakers can mimic rational agency from a third-person perspective, yet such speakers fail to satisfy the conditions for genuine rational agency.

Beyond my work with Hirsch, I took Peter Epstein’s course on mental content, a graduate seminar on intentionalist and direct realist accounts of perceptual content, as well as several classes with Katrina Elliott on causation, explanation and scientific realism.

Although I have not taken coursework directly on LLM interpretability, I had some background in computer science during my undergraduate studies. In 2020, I won Second Prize in the Asia and Pacific Mathematical Contest in Modeling, for which I implemented a neural network with grey prediction model to predict U.S. presidential election outcomes from economic data.
