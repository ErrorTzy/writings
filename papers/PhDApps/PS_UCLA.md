**Areas of Interest:** My primary interests are in the philosophy of language and mind, particularly metasemantics and mental content. Specifically, I am interested in the nature of meaning, mental representations and misrepresentations. I also have a strong interest in metaphysics, esp. ontological commitment and metaphysical explanation, as well as philosophical logic, particularly the philosophy of computation. I also have an additional interest in the interpretability and alignment of LLMs. UCLA is an ideal place for me, given the strong faculty in philosophy of language and mind.

**Previous Coursework and Experience:** During my M.A. at Brandeis, I was deeply influenced by my advisor, Eli Hirsch. Under his influence I developed an interest in the relationship between meaning, interpretation, and ontological commitment. My M.A. thesis, which is my sample paper, argues against Hirsch’s metaphysical deflationism. I argue that Hirschian deflationism relies on a questionable application of the metasemantic principle of interpretive charity, and that the deflationary move that does not invoke this principle risks circularity.

In another paper written for Hirsch’s seminar, I argued against Cappelen and Dever that the indexicality of the first-person pronoun is essentially irreducible for rational agents. I constructed an alternative, indexical-free language whose speakers can mimic rational agency from a third-person perspective, yet such speakers fail to satisfy the conditions for genuine rational agency.

Beyond my work with Hirsch, I took Peter Epstein’s course on mental content, a graduate seminar on intentionalist and direct realist accounts of perceptual content, as well as several classes with Katrina Elliott on causation, explanation and scientific realism.

Although I have not taken coursework directly on LLM interpretability, I had some background in computer science during my undergraduate studies. In 2020, I won Second Prize in the Asia and Pacific Mathematical Contest in Modeling, for which I implemented a neural network with grey prediction model to predict U.S. presidential election outcomes from economic data.
