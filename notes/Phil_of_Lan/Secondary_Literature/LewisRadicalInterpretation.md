::: The problem of radical interpretation {}

Given $P$, \[i.e.\] the facts about Karl as a physical system, solve for the rest \[i.e. Karl's beliefs, desires and meaning\]

**Ao**: Karl's attitudes: beliefs and desires, as expressed in *our* language
**Ak**: Karl's attitudes: beleifs and desires, as expressed in *Karl's* language
**M**: Karl's meanings: truth conditions of his full sentences (and denotations, etc. of constiotuents of sentences)
:::

> is it even proper to assume there is such a thing as "Karl's belief and desires"? What if Karl does not have "beliefs" and "desires", interpreted in our language? How can we fix Karl's mental states at all, if its contents are completely alien? If we fix mental states with its qualitative characters, then there is no guarantee for there to be such mental states in Karl. If we adopt this, then there is a "leap of faith" in the beginning to think that Karl's experience is interpretable by the interpretor. Or, we may suppose words like "belief" and "desire" fix their reference through their functional feature, i.e. someone believe something iff someone would produce some behavior given some enviornmental stimuli. This is dispositionalism. The same idea applies to way how "meaning" fix its reference. If what "meaning of some term 'x'" is fixed by some mental character, like intentionality, then there is no guarantee that Karl has what I would call "intentionality" -- as long as intentionality is something *irreducibly* mental. Of course, we can guarantee that Karl have intentionality if intentionality is somehow naturalized.

**Lewis's Remark**: 

1. $M$ specifies the truth condition of Karl's sentences
2. $M$ specifies a way of parsing the sentences of Karl's language

The presupposition is minimal materialism: **P** does determine $A_{o}, A_{k}$ or $M$. In other words, it is impossible to have two Karls exactly alike with respect to **P**, yet differ in $A_{o}, A_{k}$ or $M$ (supervenience)

There should be general theory about the relationship between $P, A_{o}, A_{k}$ and $M$. We use this theory to define a person: 

- Something *may* count as a person's system of beliefs, desires and meanings if and only if it is a system that *more or less* conforms to the principles of the theory
- For any human being *(with certain exceptions)*, there will exist a system of beleifs, desires and meanings correctly so-called -- that is, one that conforms to the principles

> So Lewis decide to fix the reference by the theory, i.e. the relation between them? But his way of defining it contains fuzzy words like "more or less" and "may".

This theory should be made up of common-sensical properties because beleif. Esoteric scientific findings should be kept out of the theory.
